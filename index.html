What it does

One screen: the map on the left, a planning panel on the right.

01

3D terrain

Real elevation with satellite or USGS topo imagery. Fly the route, spin the view, or color each section by its hazard rating for your date.

02

Route by section

Trailhead to summit, split into sections with mileage, gain, terrain and hazard ratings for crevasses, rockfall, avalanche, falls and navigation.

03

Plan

Choose camps, team size and pace. Get an elevation profile and a day-by-day itinerary with moving times and a turnaround time.

04

Weather at every waypoint

Hourly forecast at each waypoint's own elevation: wind aloft, freezing level, wind chill and new snow, plus the NWS text forecast and sunrise, sunset and moon.

05

Updates

Active weather alerts, NWAC avalanche danger for the zone, park alerts and curated route and access reports.

06

Gear list

A checklist built from the route's terrain, the season, your number of nights and the forecast, with ticks saved on this device.

07

Trip sheet and log

A sheet to leave with someone at home, a trailhead checklist, and a climb log of summits and turnarounds.

08

Editor

Drag waypoints, trace sections, import a GPX track, or add a whole new peak, then download the file and commit it.

Where the data comes from

No accounts and no API keys. Each source loads on its own, so if one is down the rest keep working.

  • TerrainMapzen terrarium tiles, AWS Open Data
  • Imagery and topoUSGS The National Map
  • Hourly forecastOpen-Meteo
  • Text forecast and alertsNational Weather Service
  • Avalanche dangerNWAC via avalanche.org
  • Route reportsCurated in this repository

Read this first

  • Route lines are approximate. They were placed by hand, not surveyed, and are drawn dashed.
  • Route facts are unverified. Check elevations, grades and permit rules with the land manager and a current guidebook.
  • Weather is model output. Read the NWS and NWAC forecasts as well.
  • This is a planning aid, not a navigation tool.
